So the last thing I want to show you is a small app of my own, called LiveStatus.

It’s a light link between two people who live far apart. You don’t start a conversation — you share a moment, and the two photos stack into one, yours on top and theirs underneath.

I designed it, built the front end, put it on the App Store and ran the growth myself.

What it is

Staying close shouldn’t take a conversation.

Making a moment
The problem

Chatting is heavy, and there are already ten places to do it. For a couple or two best friends living apart, the hard part isn’t talking — it’s having to think of something to say.

The move

Share a moment instead. Two photos stack into one, top and bottom — a shared frame, and a way of showing a bit of yourself without writing a message.

Where it lives

On the home screen, as a widget: their moment, on your phone, with nothing to open.

Who it’s for

Couples and close friends who are far apart.

How it was built

Solo — design, front end, App Store release and growth, with one developer on the back end and AI through every step.

The problem is pretty plain. Chatting is heavy, and there are already ten other places to do it. For a couple or two best friends living apart, the hard part isn’t talking — it’s having to think of something to say first.

So instead of a conversation, you just casually share a moment. Two photos stack into one, top and bottom, and that becomes a frame of the two of you. It’s company you can feel, and it’s a bit of personal expression at the same time — which is what keeps a long-distance relationship going.

On the left you can see it — making a moment, and then the log of everything the two of you have made. It lives on the home screen too, as a widget, so their moment is just there without opening anything.

It’s for couples and close friends who are far apart. And I built it on my own — design, front end, the App Store release and the growth — with one developer helping me on the back end.

Here are the five features, and where each one is meant to earn.

First, Snap and Share — the core of the product. You shoot from the camera, and your photo stacks with theirs into one split-screen moment. Making one is free, because that’s how people get in. The paid part is taking it out — sharing it, or saving it to your camera roll.

Second, the log. Every moment the two of you made stays in one place, and after a few months that’s the record of the relationship. It’s free, and it’s the reason to come back — the fuller it gets, the more there is worth taking out.

Then the widget. It’s the lightest version of company — their moment on your home screen, nothing to open. That one is paid: a two-up couple widget, or their moment blown up large on the home screen.

The map. You can see where they are, their battery, what they’re doing — it’s reassurance, and it’s also an excuse to start talking. The paid part is pinning your moments onto the map, so a memory lives in a place and not just on a timeline.

And the nudges — notifications, DMs, a little heart. Those don’t make money; they’re what keeps the loop going, and everything else depends on them.
